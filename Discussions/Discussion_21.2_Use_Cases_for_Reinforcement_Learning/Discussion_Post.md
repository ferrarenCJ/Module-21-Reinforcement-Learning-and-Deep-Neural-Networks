# Discussion 21.2: Use Cases for Reinforcement Learning

Reinforcement learning provides a powerful framework for solving problems that involve sequential decision-making and reward optimization. While Q-Learning, SARSA, and Deep Q-Networks (DQN) share similar foundations, each is better suited for different real-world applications.

**Q-Learning** would be an excellent choice for warehouse robot navigation. A warehouse environment typically contains a limited number of states and actions, allowing a Q-matrix to remain manageable. Q-Learning is well suited because it aims to find the optimal path between locations while maximizing rewards. This solution is moderately scalable, but as the warehouse becomes significantly larger, the Q-matrix may become difficult to maintain. In comparison, SARSA may be overly conservative, while DQN would add unnecessary complexity for a relatively small state space.

**SARSA** would be well suited for autonomous equipment operating in hazardous industrial environments. Since SARSA is an on-policy algorithm that considers the consequences of its current actions, it tends to avoid risky decisions that could result in severe penalties. For example, a robot working near expensive equipment or dangerous operating conditions should prioritize safety over finding the absolute optimal path. While SARSA is scalable for moderate-sized environments, it becomes less practical as the number of states grows significantly. Q-Learning may take riskier actions during exploration, making it less desirable in safety-critical scenarios, while DQN may be unnecessarily resource-intensive.

**Deep Q-Networks (DQN)** are ideal for self-driving vehicles. Autonomous driving involves processing enormous amounts of data from cameras, sensors, GPS devices, and environmental conditions, resulting in a state space far too large for a traditional Q-matrix. DQN addresses this challenge by using a neural network to estimate Q-values, enabling the model to generalize to unseen situations. This makes DQN highly scalable and suitable for complex real-world decision-making. Traditional Q-Learning and SARSA would struggle because the number of states and actions would quickly exceed practical memory and computational limits.

Overall, Q-Learning is best for smaller optimization problems, SARSA is well suited for safety-focused decision-making, and DQN is the preferred solution for large-scale environments that require extensive generalization and scalability.
